Visual Communication

Start With the Question, Not the Chart Type

If your question is about… Reach for… In ggplot2
Comparison across groups Sorted bars, dot plot geom_col(), geom_point()
Distribution of one variable Histogram, density, boxplot geom_histogram(), geom_density(), geom_boxplot()
Change over time Line chart geom_line()
Relationship between two variables Scatterplot geom_point(), geom_smooth()
Composition (parts of a whole) Stacked bars, or just a table geom_col(position = "fill")
Geography (where?) Choropleth, point map geom_sf() (coming Oct. 29)
  • This is the decision tree from the reading, compressed into one table
  • If you can’t say which row your question belongs to, the question isn’t sharp enough yet

Your Project Questions Have Shapes Too

  • Which NBRC counties have the highest rent burden? → geography (choropleth) or comparison (sorted dot plot)
  • Have home values risen faster in NBRC counties than in the rest of each state? → change over time (line chart, one line per group)
  • Do counties with more seasonal homes have tighter year-round rental markets? → relationship (scatterplot)
  • Which towns host a hospital, a pharmacy, and a grocery store? → geography (point map)
  • How unevenly are essential services spread across the region? → distribution (histogram of establishments per 1,000 residents)

The Code Behind the Right-Hand Figure

pov_df <- midwest |>
  mutate(metro = if_else(inmetro == 1, "Metro", "Nonmetro")) |>
  group_by(state, metro) |>
  summarize(avg_poverty = mean(percbelowpoverty), .groups = "drop")

ggplot(pov_df, aes(x = avg_poverty, y = fct_reorder(state, avg_poverty), color = metro)) +
  geom_line(aes(group = state), color = "gray70", linewidth = 1.5) +
  geom_point(size = 4) +
  scale_color_manual(values = c("Metro" = "gray50", "Nonmetro" = "#007155")) +
  scale_x_continuous(labels = scales::label_percent(scale = 1)) +
  labs(title = "Nonmetro counties are poorer in every Midwest state",
       subtitle = "Average county poverty rate, 2000",
       x = NULL, y = NULL, color = NULL,
       caption = "Source: ggplot2::midwest") +
  theme_minimal() +
  theme(legend.position = "top", panel.grid.minor = element_blank())
  • This “dumbbell” plot is just two layers you already know: a gray geom_line() connecting each state’s pair of points, then geom_point() on top
  • The gap is widest in Illinois (about 5 points) and narrowest in Indiana (about 1)

Writing Figures for NBRC

  • One figure, one message. If a figure needs two sentences to explain, it’s probably two figures.
  • The title is the takeaway. “Rent burden by county” is a label. “One in three renters in Coös County pays more than 30% of income on rent” is a finding.
  • Plain-language labels with units. No column names, no acronyms NBRC staff would have to look up.
  • Sort categories by value, not alphabetically, unless the order itself means something
  • Label directly when you can, so readers don’t bounce between the figure and a legend
  • Normalize: per capita, per household, or a percentage, so big counties don’t win every comparison by default
  • Always cite the source and year in the caption
  • Sometimes the answer is a table or a single number. A big, well-labeled “38%” can beat any chart.

Let’s Get Started

Step 1: Pick a Team (~15 min)

  • Rural Housing: supply, demand, and affordability of housing in NBRC counties
  • Rural Hub Services: quantifying and visualizing the essential businesses and amenities that cluster in central towns to serve the smaller communities around them
  • Go around the room: which domain is your first choice, and why?
  • We want two teams of roughly equal size
  • Think about what you bring, too. A good team mixes people who like wrangling data, people who like making figures, and people who like writing.

Step 2: Roles (~10 min)

A starting point. Rename, merge, or split these however works for your team.

  • Coordinator: keeps the work plan current, runs team check-ins, and is the main point of contact with me
  • Data lead: owns the data pipeline, so everyone is working from the same cleaned files
  • Visualization lead: keeps figures and maps consistent (colors, fonts, labels) and holds the team to tonight’s standards
  • Writing lead: owns the narrative and the final edit, so the deliverable reads in one voice

Everyone writes code, and everyone writes prose. Roles are about who’s responsible, not who does all the work.

Step 3: Research Questions (~30 min)

A good research question for this project is:

  • Answerable with data you can get (public, county- or town-level, covering all four states)
  • Specific: it names a measure, a place, and ideally a comparison or a time span
  • Useful to NBRC: someone could make a funding or policy decision with the answer
  • Figure-shaped: you can already picture the map or chart that answers it
Weak Better
What is housing like in NBRC counties? Which NBRC counties have the highest share of renters paying more than 30% of income on rent, and how do they compare with the rest of their state?
Are rural services declining? How has the number of grocery stores and pharmacies in NBRC counties changed since 2012, and which counties lost the most?

Starter Data Sources

Rural Housing

  • American Community Survey via tidycensus: rent burden, home values, vacancy (including seasonal homes), age of housing stock
  • HUD CHAS: housing problems by income level
  • HUD Fair Market Rents
  • Census Building Permits Survey: new construction

Rural Hub Services

  • County and ZIP Code Business Patterns: establishments by industry (grocery, pharmacy, hardware, banks)
  • FDIC Summary of Deposits: bank branch locations
  • CMS / HRSA: hospitals and clinics
  • USDA ERS rural-urban codes: which places are hubs, and which are hinterland
  • Census places and TIGER via tigris
  • NBRC’s service area is a defined list of counties, not four whole states. Get the list from nbrc.gov and make it the first object you build. Both teams should use the same one.

  • By Dec. 3: what does a complete draft include? Report, slides, or dashboard?
  • By Dec. 17: who presents what in Montpelier?
  • When will your team meet outside of class, and where will your shared files live?
